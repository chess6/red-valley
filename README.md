# Red Valley

A 3D farming prototype about **thinking like a farmer** — reading your land,
anticipating the weather, and making judgment calls under time pressure —
rather than performing farming-themed chores.

Built with Godot 4.7 + Blender 5.1 (all placeholder assets are generated by
script; no downloaded content).

## Run it

```bash
/opt/Godot4/Godot_v4.7-stable_linux.x86_64 --path .
```

Optional: `RED_VALLEY_SEED=11361` gives a nice demo run — three calm days to
learn the farm, a **frost night on day 4**, then a rainy stretch that tests
the clay field. Without the variable each run gets a fresh weather seed.

## Controls

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| WASD + mouse | Move / look (Shift = run) |
| 1–7 | Select tool: Hand, Watering can, Seeds, Compost, Manure, Mulch, Row cover |
| 3 (again) | Cycle seed type (tomato → cabbage → potato → wheat) |
| Left click | Use tool on the plot you face |
| F | Inspect plot closely (qualitative report) |
| E | Interact: talk to Sarah, supply crate, sleep at the farmhouse door |
| T | Wait one hour |
| F3 | Developer overlay (raw simulation numbers) |
| Esc | Pause / controls / quit |

**Every field action costs in-game minutes.** Watering a plot takes 8, a row
cover takes 18. A working day is finite; that's the whole game.

## What the game is doing (design)

The simulation is a small chain — **weather → soil → crop → your time** —
with no scripted events:

- **Weather** is a Markov chain over six patterns (clear, hot & dry, overcast,
  rain, heavy rain, cold snap) with a *deliberately imperfect* forecast.
  "Frost (60%)" sometimes doesn't freeze, and sometimes a clear night bites.
- **Soils** differ physically: sand drains and dries fast, loam holds a good
  balance, clay drinks slowly and then won't let go — heavy rain waterlogs it
  for days. Same rain, three different fields.
- **Crops** share one generic response model (moisture band, drought rate,
  waterlog tolerance, frost threshold, heat threshold, fertility hunger).
  All crop/soil/weather drama *emerges* from those numbers: potatoes rot in
  soaked clay, cabbage shrugs off frost but bolts in a heat wave, tomatoes
  love heat and die at the first touch of frost, wheat survives nearly
  anything but pays little.
- **Amendments solve different problems**: compost feeds gently, manure feeds
  hard but scorches seedlings, mulch keeps water in the ground, row covers
  trap ~5 °C of warmth (and shed rain, and cost light) — nothing is a strict
  upgrade of anything else.
- **Information is observational.** The game tells you *"the leaves are
  slightly wilted"* and *"the soil is dry a finger's depth down"*, never
  `moisture=0.43`. Raw numbers exist only behind F3.
- **Sarah**, the neighbour, runs her own farm with the same simulation. Help
  her (water her dry beds, bring in her harvest, cover her crops) and she
  remembers. When a frost warning lands and you can't cover everything, ask
  her — if you've earned it, she walks over with her own fleece and covers
  beds herself while you handle the rest.

### The signature situation

Nothing scripts it, but the systems reliably produce it: tomatoes and cabbage
in the ground, a frost warning at 51%, four hours of daylight left, covers
enough for half the field. Cover the tomatoes and gamble the cabbage? Harvest
early at half yield? Ask Sarah for the favour she owes you? Ignore a forecast
that was wrong last week? The morning after answers you.

## Regenerating assets

```bash
/opt/blender/blender --background --python tools/blender/gen_assets.py
```

Writes all 30 GLBs (buildings, trees, characters, crop growth stages, row
cover, hills ring) into `assets/models/`.

## Project layout

```
src/sim/        pure simulation (WeatherModel, PlotSim, CropDB, Qualitative) — no scene code
src/autoload/   Game (clock/labor/inventory), Weather, Farm (plot registry + tick)
src/world/      procedural world builder + Plot node (visual wrapper of PlotSim)
src/player/     third-person controller, camera, tools
src/npc/        Sarah: work AI, reciprocity, help requests
src/ui/         HUD, inspection, dialogue, shop, debug overlay
tests/          headless unit + integration tests
tools/blender/  asset generator
```

Dev screenshot harness: `RV_SHOT_FILE=/tmp/x.png RV_SHOT_DAY=5 RV_SHOT_HOUR=6.6 RV_SHOT_POS=-1,-6 RV_SHOT_YAW=3.1 RV_SHOT_PITCH=-0.35 <godot> --path .` (plus `RV_SHOT_ACTION=inspect|dialogue|shop|debug|states`).

## What works / what's rough

**Works** — the full loop: walk, plant, water, amend, cover, harvest, sell,
buy, sleep; weather visibly changes the world (rain, lingering morning frost,
day/night, fog, cloud); soils behave differently enough to learn; the frost
dilemma emerges by itself; Sarah physically works both farms; qualitative
inspection covers crop + soil state; 80 automated checks pass.

**Rough** —
- No save/load; a session is one sitting (deliberate cut for the prototype).
- Characters are rigless blockpeople; "animation" is procedural bobbing.
- Sarah's routing is waypoint-through-the-gate, not real navmesh pathfinding;
  she can clip corners of buildings.
- Economy is thin: harvest auto-sells, prices are static.
- Heat waves and fertility are under-dramatized visually compared to
  moisture/frost — you mostly notice them through inspection text.
- No audio.

## Highest-value next improvement

**A field journal.** The game's whole thesis is that the player's
understanding of the land is the progression system — but right now that
understanding lives only in the player's head. A journal that lets the player
pin their own observations to plots ("this corner dries out first", "clay
stayed soaked for two days after the storm") plus an auto-log of nightly
outcomes (what died, what the forecast claimed vs. what happened) would turn
inference into a first-class mechanic, and would double as the natural home
for forecast history so players can learn *how much* to trust the forecaster.
