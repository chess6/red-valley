# Hyper3D Terms of Service — snapshot retrieved 2026-08-21 (UTC)

Source: https://hyper3d.ai/legal/terms

**The page carries no version number and no "last updated" date** — only a
heading reading "Current version". Terms can therefore change without any visible
marker, which is why this dated snapshot exists: it records what the terms said
when the character was cleared, not what they say now. Re-verify before shipping.

## Clauses relied upon, verbatim

**§5(a), Rodin — output use**

> "If you use Rodin to generate Output, we will not limit your use of such
> Output, subject to any restrictions set forth in these Agreements, the
> Applicable Laws and any third-party terms and conditions applicable to such
> Output or Services."

**§2 — export rights**

> "Generate 3D Content, by uploading texts and/or images and export such Content
> for private or commercial use depending on your subscription plan"

**§5, both ChatAvatar and Rodin — IP disclaimer**

> "Please note that we make no representations, warranties or undertakings of any
> kind as to the copyrightability of any Output or the availability of
> Intellectual Property Rights (as defined below) in which it may be applied for,
> registered or credited to any person."

## Reading

§5(a) is a **covenant not to restrict**, not an assignment. Combined with the §5
disclaimer, Hyper3D grants permission to use the Output commercially on a
qualifying plan while explicitly declining to warrant that the Output is
copyrightable or that IP rights in it can be registered to anyone.

The correct manifest classification is therefore:

    commercial_use: allowed (plan-dependent)

and **not** `copyright_ownership: transferred`. The distinction is load-bearing:
permission to use is not the same as title, and an asset gate that records the
stronger claim would be asserting something the terms do not say.
